# PRISM

### Photovoltaic Recognition through Intelligent Similarity Mechanisms

**Behaviour-Informed Behind-the-Meter Photovoltaic Disaggregation for Low-Voltage Network Observability**

**Thomas Hull**
PhD Researcher | Queen's University Belfast

---

## About PRISM

The increasing adoption of behind-the-meter (BTM) photovoltaic (PV) generation presents challenges for low-voltage (LV) network observability. Network monitoring typically measures aggregate net load, making it difficult to distinguish underlying electricity demand from embedded generation.

**PRISM (Photovoltaic Recognition through Intelligent Similarity Mechanisms)** investigates whether behavioural similarity, combined with existing LV network measurements and meteorological information, can improve the estimation of hidden demand and PV generation.

The project explores how demand behaviour can inform disaggregation methods, with the aim of improving network visibility without requiring additional customer-level monitoring.

## Research Outputs

### Seasonal Similarity for Behind-the-Meter PV Disaggregation in LV Networks

Presenting at the 2026 Asia Conference for Power and Electrical Engineering (ACPEE).

This work investigates seasonal patterns in LV electricity demand and introduces a similarity-conditioned approach to selecting demand prototypes for PV disaggregation.

The study demonstrates that behavioural information can improve disaggregation performance compared with a night-only baseline, providing an initial foundation for the PRISM framework.

### Behind-the-Meter Solar PV, Electric Vehicles, and Battery Storage: A Review of Estimation Methods

Presented at the 2026 International Universities Power Engineering Conference, Cagliari, Italy.

This review examines existing methods for estimating and disaggregating BTM distributed energy resources (DERs), including PV generation, electric vehicle charging, and battery energy storage systems.

The paper evaluates approaches across different levels of network observability, data availability, and modelling complexity, highlighting challenges associated with limited ground-truth measurements and interacting DER technologies.

Particular emphasis is placed on the limited explicit integration of recurring demand behaviour within existing estimation frameworks, identifying **behaviour-informed and similarity-conditioned modelling** as promising directions for future research.

**Publication status**: Presented at UPEC 2026; awaiting publication on IEEE Xplore.

### Ongoing Research

Current research extends the initial PRISM work by investigating the generalisation of behavioural conditioning across different BTM PV disaggregation methods.

Areas of investigation include:

- Comparing estimated electricity demand and PV generation across multiple disaggregation approaches.
- Examining differences in inferred components despite similar net-load reconstruction performance.
- Investigating how behavioural similarity can inform and constrain component estimation.
- Evaluating disaggregation performance under limited component-level ground-truth availability.
